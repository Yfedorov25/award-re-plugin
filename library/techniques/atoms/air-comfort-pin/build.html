ONSEN · Mountain Bath House

A quiet order of the day

ONSEN keeps six small institutions and nothing else — water, linen, tea, silence, stone, and the night pool. Each has its hour and its custodian. Guests learn the rhythm by the second morning and stop looking at clocks.

Est. 1972 · rebuilt 202141 rooms · 2 springs

The six comforts

Everything the house insists on; everything else is optional.

Morning ritual

Bathing opens at six, before speech. Warm water first, then cold, then the long sitting on the cedar bench.

01

Towels & robes

Linen arrives twice a day, warmed on the stone flue. The grey robe may go to the garden; white stays inside.

02

Tea room

Roasted hōjicha and spring water, poured on the hour. There is no menu — the pot decides.

03

Silence hours

From two until four the house speaks in gestures. Bells are cushioned; even the kettle waits its turn.

04

Stone garden

Fifteen stones, raked gravel, one bench in shade. The gardener redraws the lines each dawn.

05

Night pool

Open water until one in the morning, lit only from below. Steam carries the day off the surface.

06

Comfort here is not abundance — it is six things, done at the same hour, forever.
