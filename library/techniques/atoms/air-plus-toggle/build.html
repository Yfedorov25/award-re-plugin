Half the journey is the mountain

CIMA flies short hops across the Alps — forty minutes, rarely more — so everything on board is tuned for altitude, not distance. Quiet cabins, food grown above five hundred metres, crews who read ridgelines the way others read menus. Press ✛ on any card for the fine print.

Six things we insist on

Services · Winter 2026
Cabin silence01
Cabin silence

No announcements above the Alps unless safety asks. The cabin is tuned to 62 decibels — quieter than the lounge you left. Conversations stay at their own tables.

Alpine catering02
Alpine catering

Cheese from the Aosta co-op, bread baked at four in Sion. A wine list of nine, all grown above five hundred metres. Served on stone, never plastic.

Night routes03
Night routes

Late departures ride the calm air after sunset. Cabin lights fall to ember an hour in; landing is announced by the smell of coffee, not a chime.

Lounge access04
Lounge access

Every fare opens the Cima Room: showers, a fire, a long table of maps. Arrive early — the espresso machine is the fastest thing in the terminal.

Ski cargo05
Ski cargo

Skis, boards and boots fly free in padded lockers below deck. Checked at the kerb, waiting at the rack — waxed on request between legs.

Crew06
Crew

Most of our crew grew up above the tree line. They read weather like a menu and will tell you, honestly, which side of the plane to sit on.

Forty minutes in the air, arranged like a good refuge — warm, quiet, pointed at the peaks.
